## Before you cold-mail me, please read this...

As academics we get many "cold" queries from people looking to work with us, either on a voluntary basis, as a PhD student, or perhaps as a post-doc. With the advent of AI, the number of these queries have ballooned to the extent that I can no longer respond individually to each message. Therefore, I have crafted an initial default response that clarifies my position. 

---

Thank you for your message and your interest in my research. I get a *lot* of these messages and their frequency only seems to increase since the advent of AI. I'm afraid I can no longer respond to each sender individually. Therefore, I apologise for sending an initial blanket response. The aim of this response is to assess to what extent your interest is genuine, but also to answer some common questions that you may have.

If your interest is genuine and our research interests really are a good match and the information provided below does not fully address any questions you might have, then please do respond and I am very happy to engage.

First, let me clarify my position with regard to relatively generic e-mails (often clearly generated with the help of AI).

- **If you have used AI in crafting your initial contact e-mail, I am not interested.** 
  - I appreciate that if English is not your first language, AI can be tremendously useful in translating and improving a message you have drafted in your native language. Nevertheless, I do need to know whether you can express yourself in English. Otherwise we cannot work together. I don't mind if your message is not grammatically perfect or free from spelling mistakes, provided the text makes sense and is properly tailored to the recipient (i.e. me). 
  - If you have used AI actually to create the content of your message (i.e. going beyond just translating and spelling/grammar correction), I'm *really* not interested. I need to know what *your* interests are, and what and how *you* think. 
  - If English is your first language and you have nevertheless used AI to write your message, go hang your head in shame.
- I appreciate things are tough out there. In order to get research experience or a PhD or post-doc position, you may have to contact many people/labs. I appreciate that this must be enormously time-consuming and a "scatter gun" approach can therefore be tempting. However, it is better to approach a small number of people in whose work you are genuinely interested and for which you are genuinely well suited. Academics are quite vain: we like people liking what we do. But if we sense that a message was probably sent (with some modifications) to everyone and their dog, we are unlikely to answer. Actually, I find it very difficult not to answer; hence this message!
- Therefore, you really need to demonstrate your engagement with someone's work and show that it fits well with your interests, background and skills. When you simply say that you are interested in `<insert words from my website or titles from some of my papers>`, then you have not really engaged with this material. Fair enough - life is too short. But you are unlikely to elicit a positive response with this approach. And again, if I sense that you have used AI as a shortcut, I am not interested.

Second, let me comment about the availability of positions and funding in my lab.

- As a general rule, I do not take on voluntary research assistants or self-funded PhD students. It is only certain kinds of people who can do this, i.e. those from more privileged  backgrounds. Moreover, I believe the work research assistants and PhD students do is real work, so it should be remunerated.
- Now, this rule is not set in stone. If you are a fully grown adult, you've worked for several (or perhaps many) years and earned enough cash to live on for a good while, but you've decided you want to do something more interesting and learn/create new knowledge rather than lose a bit of your soul day-by-day, I will welcome you with open arms (well, assuming your interests genuinely mesh with mine, etc.).
- The funding climate is really tough. **PhD funding is very limited, especially for overseas students.** As a School, I guess we have access to only about 4-8 studentship opportunities a year. Often these studentships have certain conditions/restrictions on them that mean that they are most likely out of reach for me (e.g. targeted at specific research priority areas for the university). In any event, the limited studentships are distributed over (rough estimate) ~80 academics (but often more, when the competition goes beyond the school). You can see that the chances of getting a funded PhD position are very slim. So the candidate + supervisor(s) + project proposal "package" has to be extremely strong to be competitive. When there are opportunities to compete for studentships, these will be advertised on websites such as [findaphd.com](https://www.findaphd.com/){:target="_blank"} and [jobs.ac.uk](https://www.jobs.ac.uk/){:target="_blank"}.
- Getting PhD funding through project grants held by a Principal Investigator (PI, i.e. someone like me) is no longer really possible in the UK, so that route to PhD funding is pretty much closed off. But again, should such funding become available (e.g. through grants from elsewhere), then these opportunities will be advertised on [findaphd.com](https://www.findaphd.com/){:target="_blank"} and [jobs.ac.uk](https://www.jobs.ac.uk/){:target="_blank"}, and [elsewhere on these pages](https://casludwig.github.io/Current-opportunities/). 
- As I mentioned, for overseas students, the situation is even more difficult. I am so sorry. Some countries have their own competitive studentship schemes (e.g. Chinese Scholarship Council) that allow people to go abroad for further study. You would have to apply for the grant yourself, but I would be happy to support and help you with your application *if* our research interests mesh well and we have landed on a good idea to pursue.
- For post-doc opportunities, these are almost exclusively tied to grants awarded to the PI or to the candidate themselves. If I have funding for post-docs, opportunities will be advertised through  [jobs.ac.uk](https://www.jobs.ac.uk/){:target="_blank"}, the [University's vacancies portal](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/jobs/){:target="_blank"} and [elsewhere on these pages](https://casludwig.github.io/Current-opportunities/). If you have acquired funding yourself to go somewhere and you've decided that somewhere is my lab at the University of Bristol, then great - let's talk. Or if you are thinking of applying for funding to come and work in my lab, then again I am happy to support and help you with your application *if* our research interests mesh well and we have landed on a good idea to pursue.
